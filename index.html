February 17, 2026 • Blog

Van Plato tot Vibe Coding

A thoughtful session that reframed programming as the formalization of thought. It connected Plato's questions about logic and representation to today’s machines, infrastructure and AI.

#Philosophy#AI

What I learned: programming did not begin with computers; it began with making thinking explicit. Clear structure, abstraction and precise expression are still the same foundations behind prompt engineering, AI and modern development.

Additional notes: The session showed that the core challenge has always been the same: translate human intent into a form a machine can work with. That context makes it easier to make better technical choices in a rapidly evolving landscape.

Extra reflection: It also reminded me how important it is to preserve context when designing systems — the better my abstractions, the fewer assumptions a machine has to fill in later.

#BlueTeam#PurpleTeam

April 28, 2026 • Blog

The 5 levels of Infrastructure as Code

A hands-on session with Tim Van Roosbroeck and Dimitry Decan about IaC maturity in Azure, from ClickOps through scripting to modular, governed deployments.

#IaC#Azure

What I learned: cloud infrastructure maturity moves from manual portal work to scripted automation, declarative IaC, reusable modules, and finally fully governed pipelines. Each step reduces risk and improves consistency while preparing environments for scale.

Additional notes: Level 1 (ClickOps) is a starting point, but it quickly becomes inconsistent. Level 2 scripting helps speed up work but scales poorly, while Level 3 declarative IaC with tools like Azure Bicep and Terraform brings clarity by describing desired state instead of steps.

Extra reflection: Level 4 modularity makes deployments faster and more reliable, and Level 5 governance is essential for mature Azure environments. Tagging, policies, RBAC and CI/CD give visibility, cost control and enforce standards across teams.

Proxmox VE — Open-Source Virtualisation for the Real World

#Proxmox#Virtualization

A look at how Proxmox VE enables real-world virtual infrastructure with open-source flexibility.

What I learned: Hands-on work with Proxmox exposed the pros and cons of KVM vs LXC, how ZFS simplifies snapshots and backups, and why clustering and fencing matter for resilience. I also improved my approach to networking for isolation and learned operational practices for backups, restores, and safe upgrades.

Additional notes: I practiced upgrade rollbacks and documented a concise runbook for snapshot-based recovery to reduce downtime during maintenance windows.
